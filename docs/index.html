The calibration result is the load-bearing claim of this project: Newton-Raphson implied-vol solving (with a bisection fallback for the near-zero-vega cases Newton-Raphson can't handle), run against a one-time real snapshot of Deribit's public BTC options chain (data/btc_chain_snapshot.json, 904 contracts), recovers the market's own quoted IV to within 0.3 percentage points for a typical contract (median), converging on 96.8% of contracts. The snapshot is committed so the test suite stays reproducible — no live API calls in CI.
Median, not mean, because the mean is skewed by a small tail of genuinely hard contracts — options expiring within about a day, or far from the money — where Deribit's own quoted IV is likely thin or stale to begin with. Mean gap: 2.2pp. 90% of contracts land within 5.6pp.

04

Three real bugs, found and fixed

Each caught by a test that expected a specific numeric property, not just "does it compile."

numericalT→0 division blew up to NaN
Black-Scholes divides by σ√T inside d1/d2. At expiry, T=0 sends that denominator to zero — caught by a REQUIRE(std::isfinite(price)) test.
Fix: early-return guard at T ≤ 1e-8, returning intrinsic value — which is also the exact mathematical limit of the formula as T→0, not a patch.
logicDelta hedge double-counted the premium
The hedging simulation initialized cash to the theoretical price, then subtracted the grown premium again at settlement — a single path showed −11.12 P&L against a 10.45 theoretical price. Confirmed systematic (not noise) across a 2,000-path distribution.
Fix: track hedge cash from zero and combine premium + cash − obligation exactly once. Post-fix mean P&L across runs: ≈ 0.002–0.019.
reproducibilityUnseeded RNG made the AAD benchmark meaningless
Bump-and-revalue compared a "base" and a "bumped" Monte Carlo run, each spinning up its own unseeded std::mt19937 — the two runs used unrelated random paths, producing a 3.2-unit discrepancy against AAD's delta.
Fix: added an optional seed parameter so both runs share draws. Post-fix: bump-delta 0.6363 vs. AAD-delta 0.6376 — close agreement.
05

The honest AAD finding

⚠

Reverse-mode AAD is the resume-defining piece of this engine — a hand-rolled computation tape with operator overloading, not a library like XAD or CoDiPack, specifically so the mechanism can be defended under questioning rather than cited. It's correct — delta from the tape's backward pass agrees with seeded bump-and-revalue to four significant figures — and its speed advantage only shows up once you need multiple Greeks: bump-and-revalue re-runs the full simulation once per Greek (N+1 simulations for N Greeks), while AAD computes all of them in roughly one extra backward pass, regardless of N. For a single Greek, the tape's overhead makes it a wash.

Bates: what jumps add on top of Heston

Heston alone can only move price through continuous diffusion — however wild the vol-of-vol. Bates adds a compound Poisson jump process on top (src/bates.cpp), so a sudden 20%-in-an-afternoon move is actually in the model, not just approximated by cranking up sigma.
